Category pages, product schema and speed do the heavy lifting.

Ecommerce SEO packages are search optimization plans for online stores. They focus on category page content, product structured data, site speed, crawlable navigation, control of duplicate URLs from filters and variants, and conversion improvements on product and checkout pages.

Most store owners start with product pages. Most ecommerce SEO wins actually come from category pages, because that is where shoppers land when they search "women's hiking boots" instead of one exact model.

What an ecommerce SEO package focuses on

Category pages first

Helpful intro copy, good internal links and filters that do not create thousands of duplicate URLs.

Product schema

Product and Offer structured data with price, availability and reviews where genuine, for richer results.

Speed on mobile

Image weight, apps and scripts slow stores more than anything. Speed is revenue.

Duplicate control

Canonicals and parameter handling for sorts, filters and variants.

Merchant listings

Product data that qualifies for free product listings in Google Shopping.

Checkout conversion

Fewer steps, early shipping costs and trust signals near the buy button.

FAQ

Straight answers

What do ecommerce SEO packages include?

Category page optimization, product structured data, speed improvements, duplicate URL control, internal linking, merchant listing data and conversion work on product and checkout pages.

Is SEO worth it for a small online store?

Often, yes, because organic sales do not carry a per-click cost. It works best when the store has products people search for and a site fast enough to convert them.

Should I focus on product pages or category pages?

Usually category pages first. They target broader searches with more volume, while product pages benefit from strong schema and unique descriptions.

Do you work with Shopify and WordPress stores?

Yes. The principles are the same across platforms, though the fixes for speed and duplicate URLs differ.
